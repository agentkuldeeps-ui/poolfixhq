## What you actually get, and what you do not

This is where most buyers go wrong, because Pentair sells four variants of the
same 3 HP pump and the part numbers do not announce their differences.

The **011075** is the base 3 HP unit: pump, motor, drive, three buttons, Wi-Fi
and Bluetooth. That is it.

It does **not** include a touchscreen. The drive-mounted touchscreen is a
separate accessory, part number 356159z in almond or 356348z in black, and per
Pentair's manual it can be added to any IntelliFlo3 or IntelliPro3 later.

It does **not** include the I/O relay board. That is the **011076**, and the
board is separately available as kit 356365Z.

Two things in that table deserve more attention than they usually get.

**The 1.5 HP model has the better WEF.** Weighted Energy Factor is gallons
moved per watt-hour under the Department of Energy test procedure, and higher
is better. The 1.5 HP scores 9.4 against the 3 HP's 7.1. That is not a defect
in the 3 HP unit, it is what happens when you test a bigger pump on the same
curve, but it does mean the larger pump is not automatically the efficient
choice. It is the efficient choice when your plumbing can use the flow.

**Only the 1.5 HP model can run on 115V.** The 3 HP is 208-230 VAC, full stop.

### What the I/O board actually does

It carries a 5 amp and a 16 amp relay, which lets the pump switch two other
pieces of equipment and expose them in the Pentair Pool app. Pool professionals
describe the practical limit clearly: you get on and off, not setpoint control,
so you can switch a heater but not set its temperature. You can make a booster
pump or chlorinator flow-dependent so it cannot run without circulation, which
is the genuinely useful part.

There is a persistent claim in the trade that you need the I/O board to get
Wi-Fi. You do not. The Wi-Fi radio is in the drive, which is what the external
antenna is attached to, on every model in the family.

## The warranty problem, and it is the biggest thing on this page

We are an affiliate site. We earn a commission if you buy through our link. So
read the next four paragraphs with that firmly in mind, because they argue
against doing exactly that.

Pentair's published warranty table lists the IntelliFlo3 VSF at **three years**.
Every retailer quotes that number. It is real, and almost nobody buying online
gets it.

Pentair's warranty page states, in its own words, under the heading
*E-Commerce Warranty*:

> "Products purchased from the internet, including Authorized Pentair Online
> Resellers, receive a one-year parts only warranty from the date of purchase."

And separately, on labor:

> "Labor is not covered for Above Ground Systems, internet purchases,
> maintenance equipment, or in-floor systems."

The three-year bundle terms are equally direct: *"Does not apply to products
purchased from the internet or online resellers. The products must be purchased
from a brick-and-mortar retailer, pool builder, or pool service professional in
order to qualify."*

There is a second trapdoor underneath that one. Pentair's warranty registration
card sets the **base** warranty at sixty days, parts only, and every longer
period is an *extended* warranty you have to claim:

> "To receive an eCommerce warranty, customer must: 1. Register the product,
> and 2. Provide a copy of the sales receipt."

For the full three years you additionally need an installation invoice from a
qualified installer, filed **within 60 days of installation**. Miss the window
and the paperwork does not exist to claim on.

### What this means in practice

| How you buy and install it | What Pentair covers |
|---|---|
| Dealer or pool pro sells it, pro installs it, registered in time | 3 years, parts **and** labor |
| Bought online, registered with receipt | 1 year, parts only, no labor |
| Bought online, never registered | 60 days, parts only |
| Bought anywhere, installed by yourself | 1 year, parts only |
| Commercial property | 2 years |

Pentair defines a qualified professional generously, incidentally: a pool,
home-repair or landscape professional, a plumber, an electrician, a general
contractor or an HVAC specialist all count, so long as they charge a fee and
hold a business or contractor's license. You do not necessarily need a pool
company.

On a pump whose drive is the expensive failure point and is not repairable, the
difference between three years with labor and one year parts-only is not
academic. **If a local dealer will sell and install this pump for anything close
to the online price, take that deal.** That advice costs us the referral and it
is still the right answer.

One related wrinkle if you are comparing the 011076: TradeGrade products are
contractually restricted to brick-and-mortar sale, and Pentair states that a
TradeGrade product sold online drops to a sixty-day warranty. Check the current
TradeGrade SKU list before buying any variant online.

## How it performs against our pump criteria

### Flow rate at your actual system head

This is the number that matters and horsepower is not it. The 011075 will run
flow programs from 20 to 140 GPM, and Pentair's own footnote frames the
practical envelope: filtration up to 93 GPM on system friction curve C, which
they equate to at least two turnovers per day on pools under 65,000 gallons, or
water features up to 170 GPM at 25 feet of total dynamic head.

For a typical 20,000 to 35,000 gallon in-ground pool on 2 inch plumbing, that is
comfortable headroom, which is the point. A pump with headroom runs slow, and a
pump that runs slow is quiet and cheap to run.

### Pairing with your filter

The most common self-inflicted problem with this pump is buying it for a system
that cannot use it. Owners who put 3 HP on undersized plumbing report exactly
what you would predict: excess flow they never use, difficulty clearing air
through reducers, and no efficiency benefit.

The counter-argument, which we think is correct, is that a 3 HP pump loafing at
20 percent moves the same water more efficiently than a 1.5 HP pump working
harder to do it. Both are true. **The deciding factor is your pipe diameter, not
your pool volume.** On 1.5 inch plumbing, buy the 1.5 HP. On 2 inch or larger,
the 3 HP is the better long-term choice.

### Variable speed and code compliance

Not optional any more. The IntelliFlo3 is ENERGY STAR certified and Pentair
states it complies with the Department of Energy's standards for variable speed
pumps. If you are replacing a single-speed pump of this size, a variable-speed
replacement is the only thing you can legally buy new in most of the country.

### Union and port sizing

Flexible, with a catch. Pentair advertises 2 inch, 2.5 inch and 3 inch port
options, but the pump ships with the 2 inch and 2.5 inch union kit. **The 3 inch
union kit is part number 410029 and you buy it separately.** Owners retrofitting
from an IntelliFlo2 regularly discover this at the pad, mid-install.

### Motor and drive serviceability

The weakest area, and the reason this is not a five-star product.

The wet end is conventional and fully serviceable: impeller, diffuser, seal
plate, shaft seal and strainer pot are all individually available parts. We
found no pattern of wet-end failures at all.

The drive is a different matter. It is potted, it has no serviceable components,
and the motor cannot be run without it. When it fails you replace the whole
drive, and because a drive is a large fraction of the cost of a complete pump,
the standard advice in the trade is to price a new pump before committing to a
drive swap.

### Noise

Genuinely quiet, with one honest caveat. Multiple owners and professionals
describe it as near-silent below roughly 2,000 RPM. The single substantive noise
complaint we found came from an owner running far more flow than her pool
needed, and the most technical response in that thread identified the **cooling
fan** as the dominant noise source at speed rather than the wet end.

Which is consistent with the physics and with the tech note below: this pump is
quiet when it is run slowly, and running it slowly is the entire reason to buy
the larger model.

<TechNote>
  **Percent is not RPM, and the 3 HP tops out slower than the 1.5 HP.**

  Two things about this pump's controls that almost no review mentions, both
  taken straight from Pentair's own specification table.

  First, the speed range. The 3 HP model runs **450 to 3,450 RPM**. The 1.5 HP
  model runs **400 to 4,000 RPM**. The bigger pump has a *lower* ceiling and a
  *higher* floor — a narrower usable band in both directions. That inverts the
  intuition almost everyone brings to a horsepower decision, and it is the
  mechanical reason a 3 HP IntelliFlo3 is the quieter choice rather than the
  louder one: it physically cannot reach the speeds where a pump gets noisy.

  Second, and more annoying day to day: **the IntelliFlo3 does not display RPM
  at all.** Pentair chose to express speed as a percentage of maximum. Since the
  3 HP maximum is 3,450 RPM, each 1 percent is roughly 34.5 RPM, so the
  1,800 RPM that half the internet recommends for filtration is about 52 percent
  on your drive. On the 1.5 HP, with its 4,000 RPM ceiling, the same 1,800 RPM is
  45 percent. **The same percentage means a different speed on the two models.**

  This matters more than it sounds. Practically every piece of pool advice you
  will read — turnover calculations, salt cell flow requirements, heater minimum
  flow, cleaner recommendations — is written in RPM, because every other pump
  reports RPM. You will be converting constantly, and if you copy a percentage
  setting from a forum post written by someone with the other model, you will
  get a different flow than they did.

  Keep the conversion somewhere you can find it, and note the floor: Pentair
  specifies 450 RPM as the 3 HP minimum, so the very bottom of the percentage
  scale is not actually reachable.
</TechNote>

## What goes wrong, and how often

Setting expectations honestly: the failure reports below are real but they are
not a high base rate. Professionals who install these in volume rate them highly
and several say it is the only pump they sell.

**Power surges kill drives.** The most consistently reported hard failure. A
surge or an outage takes out the drive, and since the drive is not repairable
that is a major component replacement. There is a useful diagnostic before you
panic: power off for five minutes, then power up while holding all three
buttons. They should flash magenta then cyan. If the buttons do not light at
all, the drive is gone.

**Overheating where airflow is blocked.** The manual attributes the Overheat,
Over Current and "Drive is Derating" alarms to restricted cooling airflow, and
requires a minimum 3 inch clearance behind the motor fan. Technicians have found
the rear vents packed with cottonwood and pet hair. This is a maintenance item
that most owners never think to check.

**Wi-Fi drops.** Widely reported and usually site-specific rather than a defect.
"Weak WiFi Connection" and "Offline" are formal named alerts in Pentair's own
alarm table, which tells you they expected it. The manual's own remedy is an SMA
extension cable to relocate the antenna out of a masonry enclosure — while
warning that in most other situations an extension will make the signal worse.
A second common cause is not a signal problem at all: if the pump is powered
through an automation panel or timer that cuts power on a schedule, it will go
offline every time.

**A firmware issue Pentair has acknowledged.** Pentair's own support article
states that pumps on firmware **3.03 or 3.04** may lose communication with
IntelliCenter, that the update must be applied by a Pentair representative, and
that cycling the breaker for 60 seconds is only a temporary workaround. Worth
checking your firmware version early.

**Error codes are names, not numbers.** The IntelliFlo3 does not use numeric
fault codes. The alarm list is: Weak WiFi Connection, Offline, Priming Failure,
Overheat, Over Current, Over Voltage, Under Voltage, Speed Limit, Pressure
Limit, Flow Limit, Internal Fault, Drive is Derating, Derating Alarm, and
Thermal Mode. Alarms flash the ON/STOP LED red and stop the pump; warnings flash
green or yellow. One counterintuitive detail from the manual: the Pressure Limit
warning monitors total dynamic head, **not** outlet pressure, so it will not
correspond to the reading on your filter gauge.

## Installation details worth knowing before you buy

- **240V only.** 208-230 VAC, 12.4 to 11.2 amps. If your pad has only 120V
  service, this pump is not an option without electrical work.
- **A GFCI protective device is required.** Even on a pad that predates the
  requirement, fitting a new pump generally triggers it. Note that variable
  frequency drives can cause nuisance GFCI trips; Pentair sells a breaker
  marketed as less susceptible.
- **Clearances from the manual:** 3 inches at the sides and rear for motor
  removal, **6 inches vertically above the drive** for drive disassembly, a
  minimum 3 inches behind the motor fan for airflow, and 3 feet from a heater
  outlet. The vertical clearance is the one people violate by installing under a
  shelf or lid.
- **Suction-line geometry.** No 90 degree elbows directly into the pump inlet or
  outlet, and valves, elbows and tees in the suction line must be at least five
  pipe diameters from the inlet — ten inches of straight run on 2 inch pipe.
- **RS-485 wiring changed.** The IntelliFlo3 dropped the old round proprietary
  connector for screw terminals. If you are retrofitting onto EasyTouch or
  IntelliCenter, you cut the old plug off and land DATA+ and DATA− on terminals.
- **Bonding lug.** 8 AWG solid copper minimum, 6 AWG in Canada.
- **Default program.** Straight out of the box, pressing ON/STOP without
  programming anything runs Program 1: 30 GPM, 24 hours a day. Set your schedule
  before you walk away from it.

<SafetyWarning title="Freeze damage and electrical safety">
  **Freeze damage is explicitly excluded from Pentair's warranty.** Denial
  condition 7 on the warranty card is "damage caused by water freezing inside
  the product."

  The pump's Thermal Mode defaults to on, at a 40 °F set point and 20 percent
  speed — but Pentair's own manual warns that **Thermal Mode reads the drive's
  internal temperature, not air or water temperature**, and states it should not
  be relied on alone for freeze protection. If you are in freeze country, drain
  the pump properly. Do not treat this feature as insurance.

  Always disconnect power at the circuit breaker before servicing. Do not open
  the strainer pot if the pump has run without water — vapor pressure can build
  and the contents may be scalding. Electrical connection of a 240V pump is
  licensed work in most jurisdictions.
</SafetyWarning>

## Who should buy this, and who should not

**Buy it if** you have an in-ground pool with 2 inch or larger plumbing, 240V at
the pad, and either no automation or current-generation Pentair automation. It
is the strongest mainstream choice in this class and the professionals who
install the most of them like it.

**Buy the 1.5 HP 011065 instead if** your plumbing is 1.5 inch, your pool is
under about 20,000 gallons, or you only have 115V. It also has the better WEF.

**Buy the 011076 instead if** you want to switch a light, booster pump or
chlorinator from the same app — but check the warranty implications of buying a
TradeGrade SKU online first.

**Do not buy it if** you are running older Pentair IntelliTouch automation, or
if you need SolarTouch, until you have confirmed compatibility with Pentair
directly. This is the most expensive surprise on the list.

**Think carefully if** you were planning to install it yourself to save money.
Self-installation drops you to a one-year parts-only warranty regardless of
where you bought it.
