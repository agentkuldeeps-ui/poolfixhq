Weak flow from the returns has roughly six causes, and every page you'll find
lists all six in no particular order and leaves you to work through them. That's
the slowest possible approach, because half of them can be ruled out in the time
it takes to look at a gauge.

Pump manufacturers don't organize it that way. Pentair's own troubleshooting
material splits low flow into two separate problems depending on which direction
the filter pressure has moved — and the cause lists underneath them don't
overlap at all.

## First: is anything actually wrong?

Before diagnosing, check what your pump is scheduled to be doing right now.

A variable-speed pump running a long low-RPM circulation block produces
deliberately gentle returns. Pentair's guidance for these pumps is a low speed
for extended periods to turn the water over and circulate chemicals, with brief
higher-speed intervals when you want surface movement for skimming. Lazy jets
during a low-speed block are the pump doing exactly what it was set up to do.

If you've recently had a variable-speed pump installed and the returns have
looked weak ever since, this is the likeliest answer, and there's nothing to
fix. Run it up to a higher speed for a minute and watch the jets. If they come
alive, your pump is fine.

Also worth ruling out before anything else: **is it all the returns, or one?**
If the others are strong, the pump and filter are working and you have a problem
in one line or one eyeball fitting — often just an eyeball turned down.

## The gauge tells you which side of the pump to look at

This is the whole diagnosis, so it's worth understanding rather than just
following.

Your pump pulls water from the pool through the skimmer and main drain, pushes
it through the filter, and returns it. The pressure gauge sits after the pump,
on the filter.

**A restriction before the impeller starves the pump.** It can't pull enough
water in, so it can't push much out. Less water reaching the filter means the
gauge reads *lower* than normal.

**A restriction after the pump backs water up.** The pump is moving water fine,
but it's hitting resistance, so it piles up against it. The gauge reads *higher*
than normal.

Both produce weak jets. They are opposite faults, and the gauge is the only
thing standing at the pool that distinguishes them.

| Gauge reading | Where the problem is | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Higher than your baseline** | After the pump | Dirty filter, restricted return line |
| **Lower than your baseline** | Before the pump | Strainer or skimmer basket, impeller, air leak or restriction in the suction line |
| **Normal** | Probably neither | Pump speed setting, a single return, or worn internals |

Two things that make this reading trustworthy:

**Your baseline, not a universal number.** "High" means high relative to what
*this* filter reads clean, right after a backwash or cartridge clean. That
figure differs by system, so the widely repeated "clean it at 10 psi over"
rule is only as good as knowing your own starting point. If you've never
recorded it, do that after the next clean — the direction of change is still
usable now, the magnitude isn't.

**A live gauge.** These fail constantly, and a dead one reads zero, which looks
exactly like a severe suction-side restriction. If the needle hasn't moved in
months, suspect the gauge before the plumbing.

## Weak flow with high pressure

The restriction is downstream. Per Pentair's troubleshooting table, two causes:

**A dirty filter.** By far the most common. Media loaded with debris resists
flow, pressure climbs, returns weaken. Backwash or clean the cartridge and watch
the gauge drop back toward baseline.

**A restriction in the return line.** Less common and more annoying. Something
is blocking the plumbing between the filter and the pool, or a valve on the
return side is partly closed. Valves are worth checking first — a diverter left
half-turned after someone vacuumed or ran a suction cleaner is a genuinely
frequent cause of flow that changed "suddenly."

If cleaning the filter fixes it and it climbs again within days, the filter
isn't really the problem — something is loading it that fast, and that's a water
chemistry question rather than an equipment one. [Cloudy pool
water](/problems/cloudy-pool-water) covers what puts that much material into
circulation.

We'll cover high filter pressure as its own diagnosis separately, including
media that's channeling and pressure that won't come down after a clean.

## Weak flow with low pressure

The restriction is upstream, between the pool and the impeller. Pentair lists
four causes, and they're best checked in this order because that's cheapest to
most involved.

**1. The strainer basket and the skimmer basket.** Free. Pentair's manual asks
for the pump strainer basket to be inspected and cleaned *twice a week*, which
is more often than most owners do it. A basket doesn't have to look full to
restrict flow — matted leaves against one face are enough.

Worth knowing: a skimmer sock adds filtration but also adds resistance, and a
loaded one restricts flow badly. They're useful during pollen season and a
liability when forgotten.

<ProductBlock id="skimmer-sock" />

**2. The impeller.** Debris fine enough to pass a basket lodges in the
impeller's narrow channels. This is a common cause of low flow that survives a
basket check, and Pentair's service guidance points at the clearance between
impeller and diffuser as a place debris packs into.

<SafetyWarning>
  **Cut power at the breaker before opening the pump or reaching for the
  impeller** — not at the timer or the control panel. Inspecting an impeller
  means putting your fingers where a motor can turn it.

  Never run the pump dry. Pentair's manual warns that doing so can damage the
  seals and the pump housing, so refill the strainer housing before restarting
  after any work that drains it.
</SafetyWarning>

**3. An air leak in the suction line.** Air being drawn in reduces how much
water the pump can move. Pentair's manual is direct that the suction line must
be free of air leaks and air traps. The usual suspects are the pump lid o-ring,
the lid itself not seated, and unions ahead of the pump.

If you're also seeing bubbles in the pump basket or hearing the pump gargle,
you're in territory two other articles already own: [pump won't
prime](/equipment/pump-not-priming) has the full suction-side procedure, and
[a loud pool pump](/equipment/pool-pump-loud-noise) covers cavitation, which is
what a starved pump sounds like before it's a flow complaint.

**4. A restriction in the suction line itself.** Partly closed valves again, or
a blockage in the line. Check every valve position before assuming plumbing.

## Weak flow with normal pressure

The awkward case, and the one nobody writes about.

If pressure sits at baseline and the jets are still weak, the pump is moving
water against normal resistance — so the missing flow is either not missing at
all, or it's being lost inside the pump. Check, in order: the speed the pump is
actually running, whether one return is the problem rather than all of them,
and then whether the pump has simply worn.

Impellers and wear rings erode gradually. Nothing fails, no reading moves
sharply, the pool just circulates a little worse each season until someone
notices. There's no clean test for it at home — it's usually diagnosed by
elimination once everything else checks out.

## Why it comes back

**Baskets fill faster than people expect.** Twice weekly is the manufacturer's
number, and heavy debris seasons need more.

**Valves get left in odd positions.** After vacuuming, after a suction cleaner,
after service. If flow changed abruptly and nothing else did, check valves
before anything else.

**The filter gets dirty again — that's its job.** Rising pressure between
cleans is normal. Pressure that races back up in days isn't, and that's a
chemistry problem wearing an equipment costume.

**Poor circulation feeds algae.** Dead zones with weak flow are where blooms
start, so this doesn't stay an equipment problem. [Green pool
water](/problems/green-pool-water) covers what happens next if flow stays low.

Worth being honest about the running cost too: a clogged basket makes the pump
work harder for less, which Pentair flags as higher energy use. The fix is free
and it pays for itself.

<UncommonTip>
  **Weak returns with high pressure and weak returns with low pressure are not
  two symptoms of one problem. They're two different faults with no causes in
  common.**

  Pentair's own troubleshooting table for its pumps doesn't list low flow once.
  It lists it twice, as separate entries. *Low flow with high filter pressure*
  gives two causes: dirty filter, restricted return line. *Low flow with low
  filter pressure* gives four: clogged strainer or skimmer basket, clogged
  impeller, air leak in the suction line, restriction in the suction line.

  Six causes, partitioned cleanly, with no overlap between the lists.

  Every consumer page on this topic flattens those six into a single unordered
  list — usually starting with "check your filter" — and hands you a
  guess-and-check exercise. The manufacturer's structure hands you a decision.
  One reading of a gauge you already own eliminates two-thirds or one-third of
  the possibilities before you take a single lid off.

  The reason it works is physical, not a mnemonic: a restriction before the
  impeller starves the pump and both flow and pressure fall together, while a
  restriction after it backs water up so pressure rises as flow falls. The
  gauge isn't measuring how weak your jets are. It's telling you which side of
  the pump to stand on.

## When to stop and call someone

- **The pump gargles, or the basket has visible air in it, after you've checked
  the lid o-ring and the unions.** A suction leak you can't find is often
  underground, and finding it is a pressure-test job.
- **Filter pressure climbs back to high within days of a proper clean.** The
  filter is a symptom. Something else is loading it.
- **Everything checks clear and flow is still down.** That points at wear inside
  the pump — impeller, wear ring, or a motor not making rated speed — and it
  needs someone who can measure rather than inspect. Start at
  [pool repair](/pool-repair).
- **Pressure reads zero and the pump is clearly running.** Replace the gauge
  before diagnosing anything else. It's the cheapest part on the pad.

One thing worth saying plainly, because it's where people spend the most money
for the least reason: **weak returns almost never mean you need a bigger pump.**
Weak flow is a restriction, and a larger pump pulls harder against the same
restriction — which can make cavitation and air entrainment worse, not better.
Size the pump to the plumbing, and use the [volume
calculator](/tools/pool-volume-calculator) to work out the turnover you actually
need before anyone quotes you for one. Telling you that costs us an equipment
referral. It's still the right answer.
